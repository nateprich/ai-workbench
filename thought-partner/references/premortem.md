# Light premortem

Use for a sufficiently concrete direction when possible failure deserves focused attention. If the direction is still undefined, return to intent or idea refinement.

Gary Klein's premortem method: assume the proposed direction failed, identify plausible causes, and use the important ones to revise it.

For this pack, focus on causes specific enough to change the thinking. Distinguish evidence from conjecture. Connect a material concern to an implication, possible safeguard, or condition for reconsideration. Keep the discussion proportional; no fixed quota, scoring system, domain checklist, or neighboring skill is required. Previously resolved concerns need no replay.

The user decides whether remaining downsides are acceptable. This lens may reveal a useful unresolved question rather than a final choice. It does not authorize implementing safeguards or scheduling reviews.

Method citation: Gary Klein, [Performing a Project Premortem](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem), Harvard Business Review, September 2007. The description and integration guidance here are newly written; no article text or third-party premortem skill is bundled.
