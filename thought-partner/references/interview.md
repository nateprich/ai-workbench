# Interview-me: clarify intent

Use when uncertainty about the desired outcome could send the work in the wrong direction.

Form a tentative read from the supplied context. Ask the single question most likely to change it, with a plausible guess and why it seems plausible. Wait for the user's answer. Update the read rather than defending your guess. Ask about the audience, success, timing, or binding constraint only when that missing piece matters.

When an answer relies on convention (“we need a dashboard,” “it should scale”), ask what problem that convention is meant to solve. Treat the answer as the user's account of their needs; avoid psychological diagnosis or deliberately misleading guesses.

Once the material ambiguity is resolved, restate the outcome, who benefits, the main constraint, and relevant scope boundaries in natural language. Accept corrections and ordinary assent. Return to the router with that understanding; further exploration or challenge depends on an actual remaining need. If questioning stops producing information, explain the remaining ambiguity and offer a conditional direction instead of grinding toward a confidence number.

Adapted from Addy Osmani's repository; see [provenance](../PROVENANCE.md). The original numerical confidence target, exact-yes requirement, and mandatory terminal turn are intentionally removed.
