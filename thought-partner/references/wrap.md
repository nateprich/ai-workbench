# WRAP: a wider view of a consequential choice

Use when a consequential choice risks being framed too narrowly or decided from one perspective. WRAP offers four checks; address the missing ones at useful depth and count work already done.

- **Widen options:** consider a credible alternative, combination, delay, or status quo when feasible. Do not manufacture extra options when constraints have already settled the choice.
- **Reality-test assumptions:** identify evidence that could change the preference and seek relevant contrary evidence. Distinguish supplied facts, researched facts, and assumptions. Discuss ways to learn more only where useful; running experiments is outside this lens.
- **Attain distance:** ask what a longer perspective or advice to a friend would change. The user supplies their values and anticipated feelings; do not invent them.
- **Prepare to be wrong:** surface the most important downside or condition under which the choice should be reconsidered. Use the existing [premortem](premortem.md) only if it adds useful depth. Reconsideration conditions are ideas, not automatically scheduled reminders.

Return the changed framing, tradeoffs, or conditional recommendation. Tables, dates, all-phase interviews, and a final decision are not required.

Adapted from neurofoo's implementation of Chip and Dan Heath's WRAP framework; see [provenance](../PROVENANCE.md). Mandatory phases/templates and the experiment handoff are removed.
