# Idea-refine: explore and converge

Use when the goal is understood enough to explore possible directions. Clarify only a missing fact or preference that would materially change the exploration.

Generate a small set of meaningfully different directions appropriate to the user's request. Useful moves include simplifying the idea, reversing an assumption, changing the audience, combining existing resources, or starting from the experience the audience should have. Explain why each direction exists; do not force a quota or product/MVP vocabulary onto an article, personal choice, or knowledge-work plan.

Invite a reaction where it would help convergence. Evaluate the promising directions against the user's goal, binding constraints, likely value, and what distinguishes them. Identify important assumptions and tradeoffs without pretending feasibility is established. When delegated, recommend a direction with reasons rather than forcing the user to rank every possibility.

End with useful options or a sharpened concept, at the requested depth. A thesis and a few supporting moves may be enough for an article. Another method is useful only if intent, a decision, or evidence remains unresolved. Saving a one-pager, defining an MVP, and moving into execution are optional, separately authorized work.
